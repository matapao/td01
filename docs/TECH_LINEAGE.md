# The Road of Invention: educational principles and future ideas

> Updated 2026-10-23. The active development plan is [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md): one mine, one
> workshop and one enterable shop, different crafting benches, Hand & Fire first. This document preserves the educational principles
> and a broader idea library. Some milestone and minigame code already exists; neither its presence
> nor an older example here means a feature belongs in the next phase. This update is planning only.
>
> Scope rule: later eras extend benches in the same room. References below to rivers, bridges,
> watermills, separate workshops, new regions or an electricity-first start are historical examples,
> not current requirements. ROADMAP takes precedence wherever they disagree.

The vale starts with hand tools, stone, wood and fire. Its small mine and workshop provide the
setting for the learning path. Its people retrace the road humanity took, **in summary**: the big steps that
changed what people could do, each one rebuilt in the vale for a reason and learned through a short
hands-on minigame.

There is **no finish line**. The road runs along several branches (energy, materials, making, moving,
messages, thinking machines, automation), and you go as far along each as you want, the way the
endless mines go as deep as you want. The milestones along the way pay off in the vale, and the
furthest ones mark the vale's **age**. A laptop is one possible milestone, not its end. The first
release of this revised progression covers Hand & Fire only; it does not promise endless content.

## 1. Rules for every minigame

1. **One principle each.** "Thin wire gets hot." "A little carbon makes iron strong." Never two new
   ideas at once.
2. **Purpose first.** Never a puzzle for its own sake: you wire _this lamp_ so _that tower_ sees at night.
3. **See cause and effect.** Glow, sparks, a needle, a humming motor, a cracked casting — the result
   shows the instant you try.
4. **Feel, not formulas.** Gauges carry the real words (volts, amps, degrees, bars), but you succeed by
   feel. A rule like V = I × R is written down only after you've seen it work.
5. **A why in one line.** After a success or a failure: one sentence on _why it happened_ and one on
   _where it's used for real_. Collected in the Field Notes (§8).
6. **Failing is safe, visible and a bit funny** — a lamp pops, a boiler hisses, a bridge sags — and
   points to what to change.
7. **Short and understandable.** Normally 10–30 s, at most 40 s. Include forgiving timing and
   untimed practice. A useful result and progress require understanding, not a perfect score;
   good / perfect quality is optional mastery.
8. **Principles come back.** Each idea returns later in a new shape, and the chain is the lesson:
   lamps wired in a chain or side by side → AND / OR logic; holding a fire's heat → a thermostat that
   holds it for you; the dynamo's coil → motor → transformer → radio.
9. **Do it by hand first, then automate it.** A machine only ever takes over a job you've done
   yourself; your best runs tune it (§3.7).

## 3. The branches

Future lesson examples only. Select one core lesson per later era after Hand & Fire is accepted;
translate a workshop name into a bench attachment, not another destination building.

Each line: **milestone** — _the principle_ · the kind of minigame (§4) · what it does in the vale.

### 3.1 Energy — what powers the work

- **Fire** — _heat changes things: it cooks, hardens clay, frees metal from rock_ · Hold the band ·
  braziers (exist), kilns.
- **Bellows & charcoal** — _more air makes a fire hotter; charcoal burns hotter than wood_ · Hold the
  band (today's _smelt_) · metals.
- **Water & wind** — _moving water or air can turn a wheel_ · Pick the sizes (wheel and gears) · mill
  power (the watermill exists).
- **Steam** — _water turned to steam takes over a thousand times the room and pushes a piston; pressure
  must be held, or released in time_ · Hold the band · steam pumps for the deep mines, which flood as
  you go down; steam winches. (The first steam engines did exactly this: pumped water out of mines.)
- **Generators & the grid** — _motion becomes current; wires carry it; push, flow and resistance decide
  where it goes_ · Lay the path · the village grid — the worked example in §5.
- **Batteries** — _two different metals in acid push a current; cells in a chain add push, side by
  side add staying power_ · Pick the sizes · drone batteries (longer flights), lanterns, the arc
  tower's bursts.
- **Solar** — _light knocks charges loose in silicon_ · Find the sweet spot (aim through the day) ·
  power for outposts far from the river.

### 3.2 Materials — what things are made of

- **Stone, wood, bone** — _shape comes from cutting away_ · (exists) · everything early.
- **Clay & ceramics** — _heat turns soft clay into stone-like pottery_ · Hold the band · bricks,
  crucibles, insulators for power lines.
- **Copper & bronze** — _heat frees metal from rock; two metals mixed are harder than either_ · Mix the
  ratio (copper : tin) · bronze fittings, bells.
- **Iron & steel** — _a hotter fire frees iron; a little carbon makes it strong, too much makes it
  brittle_ · Mix the ratio · steel tools, tower add-ons, springs.
- **Glass** — _sand melts into glass; cool it slowly or it cracks_ · Hold the band · bulbs, lenses,
  windows.
- **Chemistry** — _substances mixed in the right amounts, in the right order, become something new_ ·
  Mix the ratio · acids (batteries, etching), cement (lime + clay: walls that set like stone),
  gunpowder (dynamite exists).
- **Rubber & plastics** — _long chains of molecules make materials that bend and insulate_ · Mix the
  ratio · wire insulation, seals, belts.
- **Aluminium** — _some metals are locked in so tightly only electricity frees them_ · Hold the band
  (current through a melt) · light parts, drone frames. Needs a strong grid.
- **Silicon** — _sand purified into one perfect crystal can switch current_ · Separate (zone refining)
  and Hold the band (crystal pulling) · electronics.

### 3.3 Making — how things get shaped and built

- **Hand tools** — _an edge and a lever multiply your strength_ · (exists) · gathering, building.
- **Simple machines** — _lever, pulley, wheel and axle, ramp, wedge, screw: trade force for
  distance_ · Pick the sizes · a crane for weir stones, heavier builds.
- **Gears** — _a big wheel driving a small one turns it faster, with less force_ · Pick the sizes ·
  mills, clocks, winches.
- **Precision** — _parts that fit exactly need measuring and a steady cut_ · Steady hand (lathe) ·
  axles, bearings, screws.
- **Interchangeable parts** — _identical parts can be swapped and repaired fast_ · Steady hand,
  repeated · faster repairs, spare parts.
- **Assembly lines** — _splitting a job into steps in a row makes many things fast_ · Build in order
  (arrange the stations) · towers raised in series in an assembly yard.
- **Machines that make machines** — _a program can guide a cutting tool_ · Decide · workshops that
  craft by themselves (Automation).

### 3.4 Moving — getting things and people around

- **Carrying** — (exists) · the bag.
- **Wheel & cart** — _rolling beats dragging_ · Build in order · a wheelbarrow: more in the bag.
- **Roads, arches, bridges** — _an arch turns weight into a push along the stones; the keystone locks
  it_ · Build in order · new bridges and shortcuts across the river — less running.
- **Boats** — _a boat floats when it pushes aside water as heavy as itself_ · Hold the band (load it
  without sinking) · freight down the river.
- **Rails** — _iron rails let wheels roll with almost no friction_ · Lay the path · ore carts from the
  mine heads to the forge.
- **Engines** — _steam or a motor drives the wheels_ · Keep the beat · powered carts.
- **Flight** — _hot air rises; a wing pushes air down and is pushed up_ · Find the sweet spot ·
  balloons for scouting, better drones (the Warchief's drone exists).

### 3.5 Messages — sending what you know

- **Bells & fire signals** — _a sound or light everyone agreed on carries a message far_ · Keep the
  beat · an alarm bell at the keep when a wave comes.
- **Writing & maps** — _knowledge written down outlives the one who knew it_ · Encode · the map and
  the Field Notes themselves.
- **Signal towers** — _messages hop from tower to tower in sight of each other_ · Encode · a chain of
  lookouts.
- **Telegraph** — _on/off pulses on a wire carry a message instantly; a relay repeats a weak one_ ·
  Keep the beat · wave intel arrives sooner.
- **Telephone** — _a voice can become a wavering current and back_ · Find the sweet spot · towers that
  call for help.
- **Radio** — _waves need no wire; tuning picks one station_ · Find the sweet spot · drone range,
  outposts that talk.
- **Radar** — _a pulse comes back from what it hits; the delay tells how far_ · Test & observe ·
  everything on a road shows up (the tech twin of the Scrying Orb).
- **Networks** — _many machines share messages in small packets_ · Lay the path · towers share
  targets.

### 3.6 Thinking machines — counting, then computing

- **Counting** — _a bead's place says what it's worth_ · Encode (abacus) · tallies, stock.
- **Clocks** — _a pendulum swings in the same time each swing; a catch lets the gears tick once per
  swing_ · Find the sweet spot · exact timing (wave clocks, synchronised towers).
- **Calculators** — _gears can add, carrying each ten to the next wheel_ · Build in order · faster
  recipes at the stations.
- **Punched cards** — _holes in a card can be instructions_ · Decide · a loom or station that follows
  a recipe. (The first programmable machines were looms weaving patterns from punched cards.)
- **Relays & logic** — _switches in a chain decide "both" (AND), side by side "either" (OR), a relay
  that opens says "not"; on and off can count (binary)_ · Decide · gates and lights that act by
  themselves. (Relays were invented to repeat telegraph signals over long lines.)
- **Chips & computers** — _millions of tiny switches in silicon; memory remembers, a processor follows
  a program, a screen is a grid of tiny coloured lights_ · several kinds (§4) · programmable towers,
  forecasts. The laptop lives here — Appendix A shows what it takes.
- **Learning machines** _(later)_ — _a machine can learn from examples_ · Test & observe · an advisor
  in the keep.

### 3.7 Automation — machines that work by themselves

The thread through every branch: each step takes over one chore you've been doing by hand —
**but only one you've already done yourself**.

| Step               | Takes over                   | Example in the vale                                                                             | The principle                       |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Shafts & belts     | the muscle                   | the waterwheel drives saws, bellows, hammers                                                    | power can be carried                |
| Carts & conveyors  | the carrying                 | rails, then belts from the mine heads to the forge                                              | moving things without carrying them |
| Feedback control   | the watching                 | a thermostat holds the forge's heat, a governor the mill's speed, a float valve the water level | measure → compare → correct         |
| Sensors & logic    | the deciding                 | _if a lookout sees the dead AND it's night → lamps on, gate shut_                               | switches that decide                |
| Programs           | the routine                  | a station follows a recipe card, then code                                                      | a machine can follow instructions   |
| Robots             | the gathering and assembling | drones that gather, an arm that assembles                                                       | senses + a brain + muscles          |
| Planning _(later)_ | the planning                 | the keep's computer runs the base                                                               | machines that plan                  |

Your best hand-made runs set the machine: the heat band you held best tunes the thermostat, the gears
you picked set the mill. Automation frees you for the next thing you have to learn by hand — and for
the fighting.

## 5. Historical worked example: from the watermill to the village grid

Deferred reference only. There is no river or watermill in the compact plan. A future Electric lesson
starts with a small low-voltage circuit board inside the existing workshop. Do not build the village
grid, waterways or its supporting buildings to deliver the first phase.

How one milestone works in depth — and the first one to build. This is where volts, amps and ohms are
learned, because the village's appliances won't work without them.

**Today:** the repaired watermill powers anything near a pole (on or off). **Proposed:** power becomes
a budget you route.

### The pieces

- **The generator.** The river gives a fixed amount of power: _how much water × how far it falls_. A
  **weir** (crane, _Making_) raises the water → more power. The **gearbox** (_Making_) sets how fast
  the dynamo spins → how hard it pushes (voltage): too slow and lamps are dim, too fast and bulbs pop.
- **The line.** Poles as today, but the wire matters: thin copper is cheap and loses push as heat over
  distance; thick copper costs more ore; transformers (later) solve distance properly.
- **The Power Intake**, a building at the town wall: the line comes in here, and the **switchboard**
  splits it into circuits with fuses.
- **Appliances** carry a rating plate — the push they need and the flow they draw:

| Appliance       | Needs                                         | Why it's there                                  |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Electric lamp   | small draw                                    | towers see at night                             |
| Workshop motor  | big draw while running                        | sawmill, stonecutter, forge bellows work faster |
| Mine lift winch | big draw in bursts                            | faster lift, deeper hauls                       |
| Shock fence     | high push, tiny flow                          | zaps the dead along a stretch of road           |
| Arc tower       | high push in bursts (later: a capacitor bank) | lightning damage (strong against plate)         |
| Radio mast      | steady, clean power                           | drone range, outposts talk                      |
| Battery charger | steady one-way current                        | drone batteries, lanterns                       |

### The switchboard minigame (Lay the path)

A board shows the intake on the left and your appliances as they stand in the world. You lay wire
(thin, medium, thick), splits (side by side) and chains (one after another), fuses and switches, then
throw the lever. Current runs as moving dots, lamps glow as bright as they really would, wires warm
from yellow to cherry red, and meters show volts, amps and watts at every point. Any board can flip to
the **water view**: push is how high the water stands, flow is how much runs, resistance is how
narrow the pipe is — the river and the mill are right there in the vale.

The grid grows as the village needs more, and each new need is one lesson:

| #   | The need                      | What you see                                                                                            | The principle (Field Notes)                                                                                                           |
| --- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Light the first tower         | intake → lamp: it glows                                                                                 | **Voltage is the push** that drives current through things.                                                                           |
| 2   | Two towers, one wire          | in a chain both glow dim, and unscrewing one kills both; split them and both are bright and independent | **In a chain things share the push; side by side each gets all of it.** Houses are wired side by side; old fairy lights were a chain. |
| 3   | The far tower is dim          | a long thin wire warms up and its lamp is weak; thicker wire or a shorter run fixes it                  | **Resistance:** a long, narrow path turns part of the push into heat. That's why power lines are thick and toasters use thin wire.    |
| 4   | Workshops on the lamp circuit | the thin branch glows red and smokes — the fuse pops first                                              | **Current is the flow:** more things on, more flow. A fuse is the weak link that breaks first, on purpose.                            |
| 5   | Everything on at once         | the whole grid browns out; the generator meter shows watts                                              | **Power = push × flow.** The river gives so much: choose what runs (lamps at night, motors by day).                                   |
| 6   | Dim the lamps on a quiet road | a dimmer: more resistance → less flow → dimmer; double the push → double the flow                       | **Ohm's rule: push = flow × resistance (V = I × R)** — written down now, because you've seen it.                                      |
| 7   | A crusher snaps a pole        | sparks, the breaker trips, the town goes dark until it's mended                                         | **A short circuit** is a path with no resistance: the flow rushes through it. Breakers protect the town.                              |

How well it's wired matters in play: little waste leaves spare power for more appliances, the right
fuses mean no fires, and well-lit towers lose nothing at night. Bad wiring shows in the world: an
unfused overload can set a building alight.

### The story beat: DC first, then AC

- The first dynamo makes **one-way current (DC)** — a commutator keeps it flowing one way. Fine for the
  village.
- When the grid must reach the far lookouts, mine heads and outposts, the push dies along the wire,
  and thick copper everywhere costs too much ore.
- The fix is **alternating current (AC) + transformers**: the current swings back and forth, a
  transformer raises the push at the mill (more turns on the outgoing coil), thin wire carries it far,
  and another lowers it at the outpost. **High push and low flow travel far with little loss** — and
  transformers only work with a changing current. That's the real reason AC grids won.

## 7. One workshop, different crafting benches

The workshop is one small KayKit dungeon room. Begin with three visible work positions: woodworking,
stone/assembly and a small hearth. The mine and nearby yard supply their materials. Choosing a bench
shows only its available recipes and the next relevant learning prerequisite.

| Work position      | Hand & Fire use                                            | Possible later attachment in the same room   |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Woodworking        | Supported cut, planks, crate parts                         | Gear jig or hand-winch assembly              |
| Stone and assembly | Shape blocks; fit the crate's parts in order               | Anvil, circuit board, signal/control tray    |
| Hearth             | Learn fuel and airflow; sustain a fire for a small brazier | Crucible, then a small boiler teaching model |

These are activity profiles, not promises of new buildings. Keep at most three main bench positions;
reuse an assembly surface and swap attachments as the era changes. Branches describe what is being
learned. Do not require a separate profession, NPC, room, inventory or building for each branch.

Bring the recipe's materials, play one short educational activity, receive a useful crafted part,
and use it in a build or repair. Hand & Fire needs wood and stone rather than later-era components.
Guided failures return essential teaching materials. Future automation can take over a job only after
the player has performed and understood it by hand.

A co-op team may share gathering and bench work. Specialised mastery can remain a later option;
no profession system is required to deliver the first three lessons.

### Utility-only shop; research comes later

The current mechanics loop gathers materials through permanent terrain excavation and chopping.
The shop supplies the complete durable Crafter Kit, including the scanner and reusable seed pouch.
It sells no consumables, soil, materials or crafted goods. More advanced utilities can follow later
research/era unlocks; that catalogue and educational minigames are deferred. ROADMAP describes the
current excavation and planting loop before the research-phase ideas in this reference.

### Tower building and the oversight post

As detailed in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md), the workshop also has one shared tower construction platform
inside the defended yard. Bench-made parts become a completed tower there, then a ready-to-deploy
storage entry. The first wooden tower reuses Build in order to teach a stable base and supports.

Climbing the existing defensive stairs reveals a planning table/map and sighting equipment. Using
that post enters a top-down overview of all wave approaches. Select a tower from storage, preview a
valid field position and confirm; one brief transport and deployment graphic carries it out and sets
it ready. The hero stays inside. No electricity, extra building, escorted hauling or second rendered
scene is required. Later eras upgrade this same equipment and platform rather than adding facilities.

## 8. How knowledge is kept

- **Field Notes** (a book beside the plan book): an entry appears the first time you see a principle
  work — or fail. Each has _what_, _why_ and _where it's used for real_, plus your own result ("your
  far lamp got 60 % of the push").
- **The Chronicle**: one line per milestone on how humanity did it, so the vale's road is told against
  the real one (e.g. _"The first steam engines pumped water out of mines."_).
- **No quizzes.** Recall happens in the world: later minigames quietly reuse earlier ideas.
- **Guided, then free, then optimised.** The first time, a minigame shows ghost hints; then you do it
  alone; then efficiency earns better results.
- **Mastery and teaching.** Mastery per discipline (as now) speeds you up; a master can teach a
  teammate a plan in half the study time.

## 9. Pacing, threats and co-op

- Keep a lesson under 40 seconds; one small material trip and one successful batch should suffice
  for each starter lesson. No grinding or required perfect results.
- In solo play, pause wave pressure during a guided lesson. In multiplayer, the shared world keeps
  running and a learner can leave the activity safely.
- Mistakes explain the cause inside the activity. No lost buildings, world fires, or essential
  tutorial materials destroyed by a failed first attempt.
- Co-op can split gathering, crafting and building in the same compact three-building layout. Additional camps,
  siege mechanics and production logistics are outside the first phase.

## 11. Decisions and deferred questions

Resolved for the compact plan:

- One mine, one workshop, one enterable shop, three initial crafting bench positions; no river, bridge or separate production town.
- Hand & Fire first. Later eras unlock recipes and attachments in the same room.
- Painted surfaces and steady fill carry the look. Effects must stay optional and inexpensive;
  there are no per-bench dynamic lights or post-processing requirements.
- Learning mistakes stay safe. Timing mastery is optional; principles and useful outputs drive progress.
- Space travel and new regions are deferred; the era can be taught with a tabletop model.

## Appendix A — what a modern thing takes: a laptop

One milestone on the _Thinking machines_ branch, and how far back into every branch it reaches:

```
LAPTOP
├── Processor ── a chip ── silicon wafer ← pure silicon ← quartz/sand + coal in an arc furnace (Energy!)
│                       ├─ doping: phosphorus (← bone ash) · boron (← borax)
│                       ├─ printed with light: lens + mask (glass) · etched with acid (chemistry)
│                       ├─ clock: a quartz crystal that rings at an exact rate
│                       └─ gold bond wires, a ceramic case
├── Memory ───── chips of tiny charge stores · (first: woven core memory — iron rings + copper wire)
├── Storage ──── a disk: a coated spinning platter + a read head + motor magnets (rare earths)
├── Screen ───── glass + a see-through conductor + liquid crystal or LEDs · red/green/blue pixels
├── Battery ──── lithium cells (lithium, cobalt/nickel, graphite)
├── Board ────── copper paths on fibreglass (glass + resin) · solder (tin + silver)
├── Keyboard ─── a grid of switches (copper, springs, plastic)
├── Case & cooling  aluminium (← bauxite + lots of electricity) · copper heat pipe · a fan (a motor)
└── Software ─── code: instructions, loops, decisions
```

The same treatment works for any modern thing worth showing — an engine, a radio, a solar panel —
as a page in the Field Notes once you've built it.
