> Combat and repair loop (2026-10-23): road attackers start at hero height: armed undead soldiers,
> skirmishers and armoured warriors, followed by larger threats. Siege breakers push wheeled rams
> and stop to attack nearby towers; the gate takes heavier siege damage. The next wave waits until
> the last attacker is gone, then gives at least 100 seconds for repairs. The gate passage opens
> during this break. Horses wait at the small stable west of the shop: H mounts/dismounts, riding
> is 2.2 times walking speed, and a dismounted horse waits where it was left. Dismount, bring a
> hammer and gathered wood/stone/planks/blocks, then press F at a damaged tower to repair it.
> Starting the next wave recalls anyone still outside, with their horse, and closes the passage.

# BrowserDefense — how the game plays

> Current starter loop (2026-10-24): gather 4 wood and 4 stone, assemble a packed tower at the
> workshop platform, choose its position at the upstairs overview table and watch the wagon deliver
> and deploy it. The first attack waits for deployment. The walkthrough is optional and reopenable.
> Standing near a door does not switch rooms; walking through it fades the character and masks the
> room change. The mine has a visible boarding/descent sequence.
>
> Current mechanics (2026-10-25): one mine, one workshop, one utility shop. The Crafter Kit includes
> four hand tools, a mineral scanner (V) and a reusable seed pouch. Right-click exposed mountain or
> mine rock for a permanent cut. Underground, rock is built from tiny 3.125 cm boxes: a pick chips
> away a cluster of boxes, and Shift clears a wider patch. Floors, walls and ceilings step in and out at
> different depths like small building blocks. The mine has a recessed lift bay and three small
> workings with branches and occupied pockets. Surface mountains retain their smooth painted shape.
> The scanner gives bearings, distance and estimated amounts for real ore pockets in the rock;
> follow the reading, uncover the coloured seam and dig along it. A tunnel through a pocket does
> not hide the ore left in its walls. Starter blasting is prepared at the arrival bench; T aims a
> charge, and a right-click places it on an exposed face. Shovel green ground or a stump once, then
> use again to plant; pines grow after 90 seconds and never regrow without planting. No soil or material
> trading. The world remains opaque, with one cartoon visual preset. Older feature descriptions
> below are historical; research and era minigames are later work.

**The loop:** buy an **axe, pickaxe and hammer** at the Market (tools are never free) → lay down a plan
(free) → gather, process at stations or buy the parts — **G** buys a whole plan's parts (porters bring
them), or **Buy the rest** on a placed plan fills what's missing → assemble with hands-on beats.
Plans are learned per hero (study time), so players specialise.

**Our side of the wall.** The vale is ours up to the **wall across the pass**, and everything we own
stands inside it: the keep at the back; the town along the high street — the gate square, the market
square with its tent and well, the storehouse, the shops and the **workshop quarter** by the gate, and
over the river the old town with its hall, church, well and tavern, houses with their gardens, the
quay, the stables, the windmill in the wheat and the orchard; the **Port's** launch field at the town's
edge; the woods, the quarry, the mine head and the camps. Townsfolk go about their day in it (guards
at the gate and on the walk, traders, a cleric, gardeners, farmers, a fisher, the tavern's regulars —
they wave as you pass, and go indoors at night). Beyond the
wall there are only the roads the dead walk in on — and our towers. **Where plans stand:** a **tower**
(with its top, gems and devices) only beyond the wall; **every workshop** (sawmill, stonecutter,
forge, anvil, arcanist — and any to come), the Port, the hydro generator and everything else only
inside; **braziers, lamps and power poles** inside or up on the **wall-walk**. A plan on the wrong
side says why. The wall-walk is a storey up, by the stone stairs inside the gate, with battlements
and torches — from up there you look out over the roads. **Heroes never leave the walls**: everything
about a tower is done **over the wall**, from the walk (see below). **The gate** stands shut from
the start: the walkers stop at its doors and batter it (fliers go over, burrowers under) — its
strength is the bar over the gateway (and beside your lives at the top), and its doors splinter, crack
and hole as it goes — until it breaks and they pour in (lives lost). Mend it at its winch inside (F,
with wood, stone, planks or blocks and a hammer): a third of the way mended, it swings shut again.

**One tower: gems below, devices above, a top to build up.** Every tower is the same stone tower with
an archer on top, shooting from the moment it stands.

_Gem windows._ Its five lower windows take **gems, one of each colour**. Raw gems come out of the mines
— **ruby** (fire and force), **sapphire** (frost and binding), **topaz** (speed, storm, light),
**amethyst** (the arcane), **emerald** (sight); topaz shows in the walls of the mine's first chamber
(the Copper Face), the rest further out. A learned gem plan melts a raw gem into one variant of its colour, set into its colour's
window: click the tower, pick a gem from its panel, bring the parts (or buy the rest) and melt it in
with **F**. It goes in **rough** (a small matte stone, 70%); **▲** cuts it **polished** (the full
stone, glowing, 100%) and **radiant** (too big for its window, alight — embers off a ruby, frost
round a sapphire, lightning over a topaz, stones circling an amethyst, a ring of light from an
emerald; 140%), each step another raw gem or two.

| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ruby     | Ember (fire, burns) · Blast (impact bolts that burst) · Fury (+40% damage) · Warheart (towers within 8 m attack faster)                                             |
| Sapphire | Frost (chills, freezes) · Rime (the dead near the tower move slower)                                                                                                |

One **element** per tower (fire, blast, frost, storm or arcane) — it names the tower (a Frost Tower);
a small emblem of it (a flame in a brass cup, a snowflake, a lightning bolt, a rune ring, a bomb)
drifts round the archer. × takes a gem out: every raw gem melted into it comes back.

_The top._ The archer starts on an **open platform** — a wraith passing within 6 m dives at him: he
ducks and stops shooting, and stings any hero up there. Build a **Roofed Turret** onto it (he shoots
from its windows, out of reach of anything that flies), then a **Gallery Roof** (he walks round the
gallery to face his mark, covered, and sees 1.5 m further). Plans from the Builders' Guild.

_Devices._ The four upper windows take **powered devices** — not weapons: a **Searchlight** (by night
the tower sees as far as by day and the dead in its beam take 15% more), a **Radar Dish** (marks the
dead on the roads, burrowers too, and counts the wave), a **Targeting Computer** (the nearly dead first,
10% more crits), a **Relay Link** (towers linked within 15 m shoot 10% faster) and a **Solar Roof** (runs
its own tower's devices by day). Devices need power: a pole within 7 m of the tower puts it on the
grid (each repaired watermill makes 14 W; lamps take 1 W), and an overloaded grid browns them out.
Their plans open as the Road of Invention reaches them.

Enchanted parts set into a plan's parts still make each building unique.

**Workshops you walk into.** Every workshop is worked indoors: run at its door and you are inside — no
key, no wait — in a room built like a KayKit diorama (the walls between you and the camera are cut
away, nothing of the world shows outside): the **Sawyer's Shed** (logs in, planks out at the saw
bench, its blade spinning), the **Mason's Workshop** (the stone table and its blocks), the **Forge
House** (the hearth under its hood, bellows pumping, the moulds glowing while it smelts), the
**Arcanist's Study** (a crystal on the round table, runes turning over it) and the **Blacksmith's**
two-storey smithy (a stair to the loft). Bring the parts in, work them at the bench (F, the beats)
and take what it made. Run out of the doorway and you are back at its door. The anvil is also
where heroes have their **weapons reforged** (see Weapons below).

**The Road of Invention (O).** The vale retells humanity's road, age by age — Metal, Machine, Steam,
Electric, Signal, Electronic, Automated, Space. Each milestone is invented once, by hand, at its
workshop: a short minigame on one principle (mix the ratio for bronze, hold the band for glass, pick
the sizes for gear trains…); good or perfect invents it for the team, writes the idea into the
**Field Notes** (what, why, where it's used for real) and opens its plans. Half an age invented makes
it the vale's age. See [docs/ROADMAP.md](docs/ROADMAP.md) for the whole road, up to bases in Space.

**The dead on the roads are far beyond any hero** — they wear a ☠ in the target frame. One blow from
any of them kills a hero outright (armour and Shield Wall or not), and a hero's weapons and spells
barely scratch them, so you can't attack or target them: **the towers deal with the waves**. They
walk the field beyond the wall, where no hero goes. Siege types smash buildings; diggers tunnel
under. **Heroes fight the camps** in the vale and the creatures in the mines' lairs, which stand at a
hero's measure.

**Over the wall.** Heroes never set foot beyond the wall — no walking, stepping, pathing or gateway
takes you out there (a hero found out there is brought back in). Instead, everything about a tower is
done from the **wall-walk**: climb the stairs by the gate, press **5** and lay the tower's plan out on
the field below (up to **48 m** from the wall); look out at it and press **F** (or right-click it from
anywhere in town — you walk up there yourself): its parts go over the wall, and the hammer beats raise
it. Its gems, devices and roofs, enchanted parts and mending are done the same way. The dead's spoils
lie out on the field: walk the walk and whatever lies within **16 m** comes up to you; look at the
rest and press **F** to haul it up. From the walk you see **40 m** out over the field.

**The battle post.** Up on the walk within **18 m** of a tower, you **crew** it (its crew glows in your
class colour), and the archer fights harder for it — **25% faster and 1.5 m further** — plus your
class's touch: the **Knight** shields it (the tower takes half damage), the **Warchief** drives the
crew on (50% faster in all), the **Mage** enchants their bolts (35% harder). Nothing below can reach
you, and **Battle Cry** from the walk holds the dead at the wall's foot, under the towers' fire. A
tower with a Seer's Emerald that you crew sees 50% further.

## The world

- **Hollowmere Vale** at heroic scale: our lands behind the wall across the pass — the keep, the
  town and its farms, woods of big oaks, pines and birches, rolling green land, a river with mighty
  stone bridges, mountains all round. Beyond the wall the roads run out over open ground and a stream
  to the crypts, about 155 m off, out of sight; waves walk a couple of minutes before they reach the
  gate.
- **Everything close, as far as you like.** Like the ground round a war camp in a strategy game, what
  the first plans need is a few steps inside the gate: north of the high street the edge of the
  **Wallwood** (11 m), which runs on to the mountains — thicker and older (more wood per tree) the
  further in you go; south of it a junk heap (scrap, 6 m), a quarry (8 m) and, at the foot of the
  mountain that closes the pass, the **Ashdeep** mine head (20 m). The ground beyond the wall is open,
  for towers. Deeper in our lands the rocks and veins are richer (about 1.5× out by the mountains).
- **The mine (the Ashdeep).** One mine under the home mine head; the old shafts at the mountain feet are
  long given up. It is cold and dark down there: far-apart lanterns, your own lantern's short pool,
  and a few metres off the black swallows everything — ore glinting out of it, a creature's eyes
  before the creature, grit off the roof, the mountain rumbling. The lift takes you down into the
  **lift station**, a hall blasted out of the rock — rock all round and overhead, timbered portals,
  lanterns — where four rail lines leave, a wagon
  waiting at each platform under a sign: the **Copper**, **Iron**, **Silver** and **Gold Lines**. **F**
  at a wagon rides it to the next stop (a few seconds; the camera rides along); **F** there rides
  back, or on. Each line runs to its mineral: a **Grade I** chamber along it, and further on a
  **Grade II** chamber, richer. The Copper Line is open from the start (copper, coal and a topaz
  pocket a few seconds from the lift; coal shows in its drift's wall too); the other lines, and every
  Grade II, lie beyond a **cave-in** — light **dynamite** at the rubble (F there, or right-click it in
  your bag) and run: it blows the way open for good (the Dwarven Trading Post sells dynamite). In the
  chambers you **mine the mountain itself**: hack at the rock anywhere with the pickaxe (**F**, or
  right-click the wall) — every strike chips it, a few break a piece away (into your bag: the mineral
  it held, or stone now and then from plain rock) and the wall gives. The minerals show where they lie
  in the rock — copper's green and orange veins, coal's black seams, rust round iron, silver and gold
  glinting, gems as crystals — richer (and harder) the deeper in you dig, as far as each chamber's rock
  goes. Dynamite on a wall brings a whole stretch of it down. Creatures lair in old workings off the
  lines (wolves, tunnel raiders, a lost shift of the dead) and guard every Grade II (stone golems
  among them) — fair fights, harder further out, their spoils yours when cleared. The minimap shows
  the whole mine: its lines and stops, the cave-ins, the ore still in the rock, the lairs and you.
- **Day and night.** A day lasts about eight minutes. Nights are moonlit and blue (stars, a big moon),
  with warm pools of light around fires, lamps and the town's street lamps — styled like WoW / D3, not
  physically lit. In the dark an unlit tower sees up to 20% less far. The wall's torches light the
  towers within 12 m of it; further out, a **Brazier** or an electric lamp up on the wall-walk lights
  the towers within 16 m (a sun topaz or a searchlight lights its own tower).
- **Electricity.** The old watermill on the river is a broken generator — bring it gears, ingots, copper
  and planks. Repaired, it unlocks **Power Poles** (power hops up to 14 m pole to pole; you see the
  wires) and **Electric Lamps** (light the night and the towers within 10 m, 16 m from the wall-walk).
  Towers pass the grid on to towers near them. Workshops on the grid work 1.5× faster.
- **Water and bridges.** Heroes wade and swim (chest-deep, slower); soldiers and the dead keep to the
  roads, bridges and fords. Each stone bridge is one deck between solid parapets.
- **The dead walk the roads to the gate** and break in there (lives lost) — or, the gate shut, batter
  it until it gives. The HUD counts the dead still on the roads; a wave is only _cleared_ if none
  broke in.
  Grave Diggers tunnel with their heads and shoulders above the ground, so you see them coming.
- 12 waves, 9 kinds of dead (siege, burrowers, fliers, two bosses), wave affixes, a full damage/armour
  matrix (fire, frost → shatter, lightning…), Diablo-style loot, camps, chests, seven shops (some hidden).
- **Your game is kept:** a single-player game is stored in the browser every few seconds and resumes
  after a reload (Menu → New game starts over). LAN games live on the host. A game saved before the
  wall moved out comes into the new vale with its heroes (classes, levels, bags, tools, gear, plans
  learned), the gold, the storehouse, the library, the Road of Invention, Space and the run's Sigil;
  the world itself (buildings, waves) starts fresh. (Saves older still can't be read and start fresh.)

## Performance

Mediocre laptops first. One plain render per frame — no post-processing passes, no shadow maps, no
reflection probes: matte (Lambert) materials under a sky fill and a gentle sun, colour grading done
inside the materials, the vignette and hit flash as CSS. Detail is _drawn_ — painted textures and
vertex colours — and light is a local effect: flame sprites, halos and light pools on the ground
under fires and lamps, soft blob shadows under people and trees. Scenery is instanced in 64 m patches
and culled past the haze, like the one-off props (whose little parts are merged into one mesh per
material); characters are drawn and animated only when the lens can see them — each one merged into
a skinned mesh per material on one skeleton, all of them on one shared clip library per rig, and
streamed in (the heroes come with the world, everyone else as they are first wanted); the mine is drawn only
while you're down it (its rock in tiles: what's dug builds again only round it); the ground is tiled. The resolution adapts (from 1× pixel
ratio, down to 0.6× when frames get slow) — unless drawing fewer pixels buys no speed (a browser
capping the frame rate, e.g. a laptop saving energy at 30 fps), then it stays sharp. There is one visual preset; no quality switches. Geometry stays opaque when
it blocks the character, and indoor walls are not cut away to expose the hero.

The underground excavation skin uses 3.125 cm cubic blocks, sixty-four per side of a 2 m chunk. Only
exposed square faces are drawn, including across chunk boundaries; painted mineral colours and
shared rock-grain textures keep the cartoon style. Surface terrain retains its smooth sampled skin. Each tool stroke stores one compact cavity and
removes the tiny blocks within it, preserving useful digging speed and fractional resource progress.
Lift planks, metal straps, bolt heads and the pulley are batched into one opaque mesh. Flying chips
use a fixed pool of 128 pieces; at most two nearby rock chunks rebuild per frame, with a 5 ms
scheduling budget checked between chunks. Distant excavation meshes are released and rebuilt from
saved cuts when revisited. These limits target integrated laptop graphics; a real modest-laptop
hardware benchmark is still needed before claiming a minimum supported machine.
